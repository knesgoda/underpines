## Executive Summary

Under Pines is entering a social-media market that is beginning to fragment away from the dominant "algorithmic entertainment" model.

A growing collection of startups is independently pursuing pieces of the same dissatisfaction:

* **Yope** is betting on private, real-world friend groups without ads or an algorithmic feed. (TechCrunch)
* **Friendster** has relaunched around real-life friendships, no ads and no algorithmic feed manipulation. (App Store)
* **SocialHuman** is explicitly attempting to guarantee that posts come from real humans rather than bots or generative AI. (SocialHuman)
* **SpaceHey** combines chronological social networking with highly customizable profiles, no tracking and no personalized advertising, and says it has more than two million members. (SpaceHey)
* **Retro** has built a polished, private friends-only photo and messaging network intended to reduce the pressure of mainstream social media. (App Store)
* **PI.FYI** positions itself around recommendations from people instead of algorithms and customizable personal profiles. (App Store)
* **Lyvio** has gone further into deliberate anti-engagement design: no algorithmic feed, ads, likes or follower counts, followed by a literal end to the feed. (The Economic Times)
* **Myspace's** owners are publicly discussing another relaunch at precisely the moment nostalgia for older social networking is resurging. (New York Post)

This is validation, but it also changes the competitive strategy.

"No algorithms" is not a moat.

"No ads" is not a moat.

"Customizable profiles" are not a moat.

"Humans instead of bots" will not remain a moat.

Even "you can actually reach the end of the feed" is no longer unique.

Under Pines therefore needs to own something larger:

**Human-scale social networking.**

The fundamental Under Pines product is not its feed.

It is the social architecture underneath the feed.

Someone brought you here.

People have identities and histories.

Relationships have context.

Communities remain small enough to behave like communities.

There is a distinction between your home, the communities you belong to, and intimate conversations.

The network can be finished for the day.

Your profile feels like yours.

And growth is constrained when unconstrained growth would make the place worse.

That combination is the opportunity.

## 1. The Market Is Converging on the Same Problem

Modern social networks increasingly compete with Netflix and television rather than with the telephone.

The unit of value changed from:

> person → person

to:

> content → viewer

Reddit conversations repeatedly describe this shift. Users complain that posts from friends have been replaced by recommendations and entertainment, describe Facebook feeds as being full of AI or bot material, and actively search for ways to strip recommendations and endless feeds out of existing apps. (Reddit)

A recent r/socialmedia discussion asking what people wanted from a new platform produced requests for chronological ordering, less algorithmic pressure, fewer vanity metrics and no advertising. (Reddit)

That does not prove that millions of people will automatically join a new social network.

The cold-start problem remains enormous.

But it does establish a clear dissatisfaction:

> People still want online social connection.
> They increasingly dislike the machinery surrounding it.

That distinction matters.

Under Pines should not market itself as:

> "Quit social media."

It should offer:

> "Social media can feel different."

## 2. Competitive Map

### Yope

**Threat: ★★★★★**

Yope is presently the competitor I would watch most carefully.

It raised $12.3 million in July 2026 while promoting private social networking without advertising or an algorithmic feed. Accounts are private by default and the company is targeting real-world friend relationships rather than public creator audiences. (TechCrunch)

More importantly, Yope is expanding beyond basic photo sharing.

Its publicly discussed roadmap includes profile customization, interests, favorite music, wallpapers and mini-games. (TechCrunch)

That begins encroaching on some of Under Pines' personality-driven territory.

**What Yope owns:** Private friend groups.

**What Under Pines should own instead:** A trusted society.

Yope is fundamentally trying to make private sharing better.

Under Pines should create an entire social environment.

---

## 3. Friendster

**Threat: ★★★★☆**

The new Friendster launched with one of the most aggressive trust mechanisms in mainstream consumer social:

Users were expected to establish friendships by physically tapping phones together. Its public positioning emphasizes real-life friends rather than follower graphs, with no ads or algorithmic feed manipulation. (App Store)

This is exceptionally important validation for Under Pines.

Friendster independently reached the conclusion that:

> The connection itself needs provenance.

But Friendster also demonstrates the danger.

Users immediately complained that physical proximity made reconnecting with distant friends or relatives difficult. And by July, Friendster updates were already introducing friend search and "People you may know." (App Store)

That is the trap.

**Too little friction:** Bots, spam, strangers and growth hacking overwhelm the network.

**Too much friction:** Nobody has anyone to talk to.

Under Pines' invite tree is potentially a much better compromise.

Kevin doesn't need to physically stand next to Taylor.

Someone simply needs to have genuinely brought Taylor into the forest.

---

## 4. SocialHuman

**Threat: ★★★★☆**

SocialHuman is the competitor that most directly attacks the bot/AI problem.

It describes itself as a "human-only" network.

Its system restricts posts to camera capture, examines sensor/device/metadata signals, blocks paste or programmatic input and assigns visible trust scores that increase over time. (SocialHuman)

That is serious product thinking.

It also tells us something strategically important:

> Under Pines cannot build its brand around "we don't have bots."

Someone else can make the same claim.

SocialHuman's limitation is equally interesting.

Because authenticity is verified at the content-capture level, users cannot simply upload an old photograph. The camera restriction is intentionally fundamental to the system. (SocialHuman)

Under Pines should verify **people**, rather than attempting to prove that every individual piece of content was created in a particular way.

Someone should still be able to post:

* an old family photo
* artwork
* a scanned concert ticket
* a meme
* a picture from 1997
* their kid's drawing
* something funny they found
* a screenshot
* a photograph from another camera

The core promise should be:

> You know who you're interacting with.

Not:

> Everything they post was photographed three seconds ago.

---

## 5. SpaceHey

**Threat: ★★★★☆**

SpaceHey remains the closest aesthetic relative.

Its current product includes:

* custom HTML/CSS profiles
* blogs
* bulletins
* forums
* groups
* messaging
* chronological social behavior
* no tracking
* no algorithms
* no personalized ads

It reports more than two million members. (SpaceHey)

SpaceHey is valuable evidence that people don't merely tolerate customization.

They actively do things with it.

**Customization is content. Building the profile is gameplay.**

SpaceHey's development history also contains repeated improvements to spam detection and bot prevention, illustrating exactly what happens when an open nostalgic network attracts abuse. (SpaceHey)

This is why the Cabin matters so much.

Under Pines shouldn't copy the old MySpace concept of:

> PROFILE PAGE

It should turn it into:

> MY PLACE.

The Cabin should feel inhabited.

---

## 6. Retro

**Threat: ★★★☆☆**

Retro is extraordinarily good at one part of this problem.

It is a private weekly photo journal designed around sharing with actual friends without the pressure of conventional social media. Friend lists and likes are private, and the product now includes group albums and group messaging. (App Store)

Retro is elegant.

But it is intentionally narrow.

Under Pines is broader:

> Retro records your life with friends.
> Under Pines is somewhere friends can hang out.

That difference should be protected.

---

## 7. Locket

**Threat: ★★★☆☆**

Locket has substantial scale among younger users and positions itself around private sharing among close friends. It also emphasizes proactive safety systems and AI-assisted moderation. (PR Newswire)

Its core interaction remains highly specific: photos delivered between close friends, particularly through the phone's home screen. (Google Play)

Locket proves there is enormous appetite for lower-stakes friend-to-friend interaction.

It does not yet replicate the full community architecture of Under Pines.

---

## 8. PI.FYI

**Threat: ★★★☆☆**

PI.FYI has unusually strong cultural alignment with what we're trying to create.

Its public positioning asks whether users miss the old internet and promotes recommendations from friends rather than algorithmic discovery. It offers profile themes, lists, community interaction and no advertising in the app. (App Store)

But PI.FYI is also an excellent warning.

Recent releases include:

* daily check-ins
* credits
* profile unlocks
* boosts
* daily games
* shared streaks

(App Store)

None of those features are inherently evil.

But together they demonstrate how quickly an anti-doomscrolling social product can begin reconstructing the engagement machinery it was created to escape.

Under Pines should be extremely suspicious of:

> streaks, points, levels, daily rewards, leaderboards, engagement bonuses, unlock-by-activity systems

Those mechanics change the user's reason for returning.

We want:

> "I wonder what my friends are doing."

Not:

> "I can't lose my 193-day streak."

---

## 9. Lyvio

**Threat: ★★★☆☆**

Lyvio is strategically important because it has already implemented an idea very close to something we considered unusually distinctive.

It has:

* chronological feeds
* no advertising
* no likes
* no follower counts
* no endless recommendation feed

And when the user reaches the end, the product tells them that they've seen everything and the remainder is offline. (The Economic Times)

That's excellent design.

We should still build our finite feed.

But we cannot position the existence of an ending as proprietary.

Instead:

> Under Pines' ending should be one manifestation of the broader human-scale philosophy.

---

## 10. noplace

**Threat: ★★☆☆☆**

noplace demonstrates demand for playful identity.

Profiles emphasize music, interests, mood and customization, and the experience has frequently been compared to MySpace. (App Store)

It also demonstrates potential dangers around gamification and moderation. App Store users have discussed levels/activity incentives as well as concerns around age separation and moderation. (App Store)

Under Pines should borrow the joy.

Not the engagement loop.

---

## 11. Myspace Relaunch

**Threat: ★★★★☆ — potentially ★★★★★**

Myspace has the one competitive advantage nobody can build:

> Myspace.

The brand itself contains enormous cultural memory.

Its current owners have publicly discussed trying to relaunch the product, although no firm launch date has been announced. (New York Post)

Current cultural coverage surrounding the possible revival explicitly connects it to growing interest in smaller, messier and less performative online environments. (Vogue)

If Myspace returns with:

* Top Friends
* customizable profiles
* music
* chronology
* messaging
* groups

it will generate enormous attention.

We should assume they will.

But Myspace's advantage is also its limitation.

Myspace has to resurrect Myspace.

Under Pines gets to invent what comes after it.

We shouldn't compete by being:

> "New Myspace."

We should become:

> "The thing people hoped the old internet would eventually turn into."

## 13. Under Pines' Actual Moat

Features can be copied.

**Language + architecture + norms** are much harder to copy together.

Our architecture is:

### Cabin

Me. My place. My personality. My friends. My history. My music.

The part of the internet that I decorate instead of optimize.

### Camps

Us. People connected by an interest.

Wine. Roller coasters. Horror movies. Gardening. A town. A band. Photography. A weird hobby fourteen people care deeply about.

Large enough to discover someone.

Small enough to recognize people.

### Campfires

A few of us.

Small conversations. Private. Temporary when appropriate.

Intimate enough that people actually talk rather than broadcast.

### Lantern

Something happened while you were gone.

Not:

> COME BACK RIGHT NOW OR YOU'RE MISSING OUT.

Notifications should inform rather than summon.

### The Forest

The network itself.

And crucially:

> **Everyone entered the forest through another human.**

That is where I believe the real differentiation lies.

## 14. The Seven Things We Cannot Let Competitors Beat Us To

### 1. Make Invitation Lineage a First-Class Product Object

Do not simply store:

> `invite_code`

Store the relationship.

Conceptually:

> Person A → invited Person B → invited Person C

Every account has provenance. Every invite has history. Every account has an entry point into Under Pines.

This supports:

* abuse investigation
* suspicious cluster identification
* invitation limits
* compromised-account detection
* reputation
* moderation
* community history

The invitation system isn't a marketing mechanism.

**It is the root of the trust architecture.**

That distinction is critical.

### 2. Build Progressive Human Verification

Do not rely entirely on one magic verification technique.

SocialHuman's approach demonstrates how sophisticated technical verification can become. (SocialHuman)

Under Pines should instead use layered confidence. For example:

* **Layer 1** — Valid invitation
* **Layer 2** — Confirmed email
* **Layer 3** — Device/browser risk signals
* **Layer 4** — Phone verification when appropriate
* **Layer 5** — Behavioral trust signals
* **Layer 6** — Community reports
* **Layer 7** — Human administrative review

A trusted user who has behaved normally for three years should not constantly prove they are human.

**Trust should accumulate.**

### 3. Do Not Compromise the Social Graph to Solve Cold Start

Friendster's evolution is our warning.

When users join and see emptiness, the easiest product response is:

> People You May Know!

Then: Suggested Accounts! Then: Popular Near You! Then: Trending!

Then suddenly we're home.

Under Pines should solve cold start differently.

The person who invites you should ideally help bring you into an existing social pocket.

A new user shouldn't enter an empty forest.

**They should arrive at a campsite.**

### 4. Make Cabins Ridiculously Personal

Competitors can clone a customizable profile.

Our response should not be more CSS.

It should be **emotional ownership**.

A Cabin should gradually become recognizable as someone's place.

Possible customization includes:

* layouts
* wall treatments
* themes
* pinned photographs
* favorite objects
* books
* media
* music presence
* visitor notes
* personal collections
* badges that reflect actual participation rather than grinding
* seasonal decoration
* messenger skin
* status
* little pieces of history accumulated over time

Opening Taylor's Cabin should feel different from opening Kevin's.

Not because Taylor changed a background color.

Because Taylor lives there.

### 5. Own Human-Scale Communities

Reddit is wonderful at subject communities.

Discord is wonderful at ongoing group conversation.

Facebook Groups are still useful enough that even people who dislike Facebook report keeping accounts for community groups. (Reddit)

Under Pines needs the bridge.

Camps shouldn't become massive broadcasting channels.

Campfires shouldn't become 50,000-person Discord servers.

As communities grow, the system should prefer division into coherent smaller spaces over infinite scaling.

A 78-person woodworking Camp may be better than a 780,000-person woodworking content feed.

That is counterintuitive in Silicon Valley terms.

That's why it might work.

### 6. Give Social Media an Ending

Lyvio proves we're not alone here. (The Economic Times)

Good. We should normalize it.

When there is nothing meaningful left:

**stop.**

No recommended content. No trending rage bait. No random viral video. No "because you watched…" No magically materializing second feed.

Something warm:

> The pines are quiet. You're all caught up.

And then maybe:

> Close Under Pines

That's not a failure state.

**That's successful product use.**

### 7. Never Create an Economy of Attention

This one should be constitutional.

Do not sell:

* reach
* ranking
* boosted posts
* attention
* influence
* follower growth
* priority placement
* algorithmic advantage

Do not accidentally create:

* influencer status
* engagement farming
* streak farming
* point farming
* engagement pods
* artificial virality

noplace and PI.FYI show how naturally gamification mechanics appear even in products inspired by a friendlier internet. (App Store)

**Under Pines should reward belonging, not performance.**

## 15. The Biggest Product Risk: The Empty Forest

The greatest danger isn't Facebook. It isn't Myspace. It isn't Yope.

It's:

> "This is cool. None of my friends are here."

That sentence has killed social networks for twenty years.

Invite-only makes this more dangerous.

We therefore shouldn't measure launch success primarily by **total registered accounts**.

We should measure:

* **Social density** — What percentage of new users have several meaningful connections?
* **First-week conversation** — Did they actually communicate with somebody?
* **Camp participation** — Did they find somewhere they belong?
* **Invitation health** — Are people bringing people they genuinely know?
* **Return without addiction** — Do people voluntarily come back even though we aren't harassing them?

That is a fundamentally different growth model.

---

## 16. Another Risk: Invite Cartels

Invite-only does not automatically mean human-only.

A bad actor can invite another bad actor. That actor can invite ten more. Eventually you have a bot branch.

Which means the system needs to understand **branches**.

If twenty suspicious accounts trace back through the same invitation lineage, that is meaningful.

Possible responses should include:

* temporarily freezing new invitations from a branch
* step-up verification
* reviewing inviter behavior
* removing obviously synthetic descendants
* limiting invitation privileges without immediately banning legitimate users
* restoring privileges after review

This is where Under Pines can become genuinely difficult to attack at scale.

Bot farms thrive on cheap account creation.

Our goal isn't to make bot creation mathematically impossible.

**It is to make scaling abuse economically miserable.**

---

## 17. Do Not Turn Trust Into a Public Social Score

This is where I would deliberately diverge from SocialHuman.

SocialHuman publicly displays a trust score. (SocialHuman)

Under Pines should probably maintain substantial trust information internally without displaying:

> Kevin — Trust Score 94

The second we expose that number, users will optimize it.

Now we've created: **karma.**

Then people will ask: "How do I increase my score?"

Then Reddit happens.

Use trust for:

* security
* invitation privileges
* fraud detection
* moderation context

But don't turn being trustworthy into a game.

## 18. Positioning

The weakest positioning available to us is:

> A social network without algorithms.

Everyone is saying it.

Another weak one:

> Social media for real friends.

Crowded territory.

Another:

> The new Myspace.

We're immediately subordinate to someone else's brand.

Another:

> Bot-free social media.

Increasingly crowded and almost impossible to promise absolutely.

### Recommended Category

**Human-Scale Social**

Under Pines is a:

> Human-scale social network built around people, not content.

That gives us room to explain everything else.

Human-scale means:

* people bring people
* communities have boundaries
* feeds end
* profiles are personal
* conversations don't need an audience
* numbers aren't the point
* algorithms don't manufacture attention
* growth doesn't automatically outrank quality
* users aren't inventory
* being offline isn't churn

## 20. Internal Product Principle

Every major feature proposal should have to answer one question:

> **Does this help people know each other better, or does it merely increase engagement?**

If the primary answer is "engagement," the default answer should be: **don't build it.**

That doesn't mean engagement is bad.

People chatting with friends for four hours is engagement.

People spending four hours unable to escape an automatically generated feed is something else entirely.

The business needs to understand the difference.

## 21. Competitive Defensibility

Facebook can copy Cabins.

Instagram can make a chronological friends mode.

Yope can add interest communities.

Friendster can add customization.

Myspace can restore Top 8.

SocialHuman can add invitations.

Anyone can remove follower counts.

Anyone can put an end screen on a feed.

What is much harder to clone is a mature network where:

> every participant entered through an accountable social graph, communities developed around explicit norms, user identities accumulated history, people formed relationships inside recognizable places, and the entire product culture actively resists converting attention into currency.

That's the moat.

Not software.

**Culture encoded in software.**

## 23. What We Should NOT Copy

No matter how successful competitors become:

* No infinite recommendations.
* No public follower race.
* No engagement leaderboard.
* No streaks.
* No points for posting.
* No paid boosts.
* No viral trending page.
* No creator caste.
* No "content strategy."

People should not have to develop a fucking publishing strategy to talk to their friends.

That sentence should probably appear somewhere in the internal company handbook.

## 24. What Under Pines Is Actually Selling

Not nostalgia. Nostalgia gets someone's attention once.

Not privacy. Privacy is expected.

Not no-algorithm. Competitors already offer it.

Not customization. That's a feature.

Not even bot prevention. That's infrastructure.

Under Pines is selling a feeling:

> "I know these people."

And immediately after that:

> "I like being here."

And finally:

> "Okay. I'm caught up. See you tomorrow."

**That is the product.**

## Final Strategic Thesis

The current alternative-social market is accidentally conducting a giant distributed experiment.

One company is testing private friends. One is testing verified humans. One is testing nostalgia. One is testing personalization. One is testing finite feeds. One is testing real-world relationships. One is testing human recommendations. One is testing algorithm-free social.

Under Pines should not try to win any one of those experiments.

**It should assemble the answer.**

The opportunity is to create a social network whose architecture assumes from the beginning that:

> humans are finite, friendships are contextual, communities need boundaries, identity should be expressive, trust has history, attention should not be harvested, and a healthy social network does not need to consume the entire day.

That is substantially more defensible than:

> "We're bringing back Myspace."

Under Pines isn't about bringing the old internet back.

**It's about continuing from the point where the old internet took the wrong turn.**
